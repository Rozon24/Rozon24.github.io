Project 01 · Fixed Income

Bond Price & Yield Analysis

Explore how the required market yield changes the price of a five-year bond with a $1,000 face value and a 5% annual coupon.

With a 6% required yield, the bond price is $957.88.
Method & findings

The annual coupon is $1,000 × 5% = $50. Discount each coupon and the principal repayment at the required yield.

Price = Σ[t=1 to 5] $50/(1+y)^t + $1,000/(1+y)^5

  • At a 4% yield: $1,044.52 — above face value.
  • At a 5% yield: $1,000.00 — at face value.
  • At a 6% yield: $957.88 — below face value.

Key finding: Bond prices fall as required yields rise because future payments are discounted more heavily.

Assumes annual coupons, valuation on a coupon date, no default, and no embedded options. Tools: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, present value analysis.

Project 02 · Corporate Finance

Investment NPV Analysis

Evaluate a hypothetical $10,000 equipment investment that generates $3,000 in net cash flow at the end of each year for five years.

At a 10% discount rate, NPV is +$1,372.36.
Method & findings

Discount the five annual net cash flows, then subtract the initial investment paid today.

NPV = −$10,000 + Σ[t=1 to 5] $3,000/(1+r)^t

  • Initial investment: $10,000.
  • Present value of inflows at 10%: $11,372.36.
  • Net present value at 10%: +$1,372.36.

Key finding: The project has a positive NPV at 10%, so it meets the NPV acceptance rule under these assumptions. Raising the required return reduces NPV.

Assumes fixed year-end net cash flows, no salvage value, and no additional cash flows. Taxes and other costs are assumed already reflected in the net cash flows. Tools: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, discounted cash flow analysis.
